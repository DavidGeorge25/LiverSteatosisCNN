Hepatocyte ballooning · mouse MASH

We need a pathologist to tell us which of these are real

Software found cells that look enlarged and pale compared with their neighbours. It has no idea which are actually ballooned. That judgment is the ask.

Subject line Ballooning review — 1 hour, ~450 cells, yes/no/unsure

What they'd be doing

One cell at a time, outlined in its surrounding tissue, in a browser. For each:

Yballooned Nnot ballooned ?can't tell from this image

No grading, no counting, no slide-level score. About an hour.

What the candidates look like

The set spans the full range on purpose — not just the obvious ones. Roughly a third are cells the software ranked highly, a third borderline, and the rest cells it ranked low or rejected outright.

Ask them these four, before they start

1

Can you judge this from this image?

If they want to pan or zoom, the crop is too small — and every verdict after that is a guess. Fixable in minutes, painful to discover after an hour.

2

Is the red outline helping, or prejudging it for you?

Both versions exist, marked and unmarked. Pick one and stay with it.

3

Is "is this cell ballooned" even the right question?

NASH‑CRN grades whole sections, not individual cells. If they don't call cells, we need to know now.

4

What fraction of these look plausible to you?

Almost none means we're aimed wrong. Almost all means we're only showing easy ones.

And one question afterwards

“When you rejected something, what was usually wrong with it?” This is worth more than the labels. Just normal hepatocytes means the ranking is wrong. Out of focus means the images are wrong. At the edge of the section means the tissue detection is wrong. Each points somewhere completely different.

A high rejection rate is the system working, not failing — low-ranked and rejected cells are in the set deliberately. “Unsure” is a real answer and is excluded from training rather than forced into a category.
